MLB's Audience Segmentation Overview

Answers the questions: what is segmentation within MLB? What role does AEP play at MLB? How does CDP enable real-time marketing? Why does MLB need a CDP?

MLB uses Adobe Experience Platform (AEP) and Real-Time Customer Data Platform (RTCDP) to create a unified, actionable view of fan data that powers personalized, omnichannel marketing at scale.

Historically, fan data existed across multiple systems (ticketing platforms, email service providers, and digital engagement tools) making it challenging for Clubs to build consistent audiences or respond quickly to fan behavior. By centralizing and standardizing fan data within a shared CDP environment, MLB enables Clubs to move beyond static lists and manual processes toward dynamic, scalable audience activation.

Together, AEP, CDP, and segmentation form the foundation of MLB's modern fan engagement strategy:

  • AEP collects, standardizes, and stores unified fan data.
  • RTCDP enables audience segmentation, evaluation, and activation.
  • Segmentation allows Clubs to define dynamic audiences based on fan attributes and behaviors.

Core Concepts

Customer Data Platform (CDP)
A centralized system that unifies fan data from multiple sources into a single, persistent profile used for segmentation and activation.

Adobe Experience Platform (AEP)
The foundational data layer that ingests, standardizes, and stores fan data from MLB and Club systems.

Real-Time Customer Data Platform (RTCDP)
The activation layer built on AEP that enables marketers to define audiences, evaluate them in near real time or on a schedule, and activate them across marketing destinations.

Fan Profile
A unified representation of a fan, combining attributes (such as season ticket holder status) and behavioral data (such as ticket purchases or digital engagement) into a single profile.

Segmentation
The process of defining groups of fans based on shared attributes and behaviors.

Audience
The output of segmentation: fan profiles that meet specified criteria and are eligible for activation.

Attributes and Events
Attributes describe a fan. Events capture actions a fan takes.

Activation
The process of sending an audience to marketing platforms to deliver messaging.

Destinations
External marketing platforms where audiences are activated, such as paid media or social platforms.

How the System Works

Longform (numbered)

Data Collection and Profile Creation (AEP)

AEP serves as the system of record for MLB fan data. It ingests data from MLB and Club systems, including:

  • Ticketing platforms
  • Email service providers
  • Digital engagement sources
  • Other fan interaction systems

As data is ingested, it is mapped to standardized schemas that define how fan information is structured. This ensures that data from different systems can be accurately combined. The result is a continuously updated, unified fan profile for each individual.

By centralizing fan data within AEP, MLB:

  • Reduces duplication
  • Improves data quality
  • Creates consistency across Clubs
  • Establishes a scalable foundation for personalization

Segmentation and Audience Creation (RTCDP)

RTCDP builds on the unified profiles stored in AEP. Marketers use segmentation to define groups of fans based on attributes and events. These rules determine which fans qualify for an audience at any given time. Segmentation logic can be reused across campaigns and destinations, creating a consistent and scalable approach to audience management.

Instead of relying on manual list pulls or static spreadsheets, Clubs create dynamic audiences that update automatically as fan data changes.

Audience Evaluation and Real-Time Updates

Once segmentation rules are defined, audiences are evaluated either:

  • On a scheduled basis
  • In near real time as new data is ingested

As fan behavior changes (when a fan purchases a ticket, for example), that fan may automatically enter or exit relevant audiences. This ensures campaigns always reflect the most current information available without requiring manual updates.

Activation to Marketing Destinations

After evaluation, audiences can be activated to external marketing destinations, including:

  • Email platforms
  • Push notifications
  • Paid media channels
  • Social media platforms

Because audience membership updates automatically, activation remains aligned with real fan behavior. This improves targeting accuracy, increases operational efficiency, and enhances the overall fan experience.

Why Segmentation Matters for MLB Clubs

By combining AEP, RTCDP, and segmentation, MLB provides Clubs with:

  • A shared, unified data foundation
  • Dynamic audiences that update automatically
  • Reduced reliance on one-off list pulls
  • Faster response to fan behavior
  • More advanced personalization capabilities
  • Improved measurement and decision-making

This approach balances consistency and flexibility:

  • Clubs benefit from a standardized data model and shared infrastructure.
  • Clubs retain the flexibility to tailor campaigns to local strategies and market needs.

Segmentation supports a wide range of use cases reducing manual effort and improving marketing consistency across seasons.

The Shift from Static to Dynamic Marketing

The move to a CDP-powered environment represents a fundamental evolution in how MLB Clubs engage fans.

Before:

  • Data siloed across systems
  • Manual list pulls
  • Static audiences
  • Delayed campaign updates

Now:

  • Unified fan profiles
  • Centralized segmentation logic
  • Automatically updating audiences
  • Scheduled or near real-time evaluation
  • Scalable activation across channels

This shift improves marketing efficiency while delivering more relevant, timely experiences to fans.

MLB's Data Ecosystem & Architecture

Answers the questions: what systems contribute data to MLB? How is data organized across MLB? How does fan data move end-to-end?

MLB's data ecosystem defines how fan data is generated, structured, unified, and activated across the league and individual Clubs. It connects upstream data sources, centralized data platforms, and downstream marketing systems into a coordinated framework that supports segmentation and personalized marketing at scale.

While the data ecosystem describes the systems that generate and use data, data architecture defines how that data is structured and modeled to ensure consistency. Together, they enable a reliable end-to-end data flow that transforms fan interactions into actionable audiences.

Adobe Experience Platform (AEP) and Real-Time Customer Data Platform (RTCDP) serve as the central layers within this framework, enabling unified profiles, segmentation, and marketing activation.

Understanding this integrated structure helps define:

  • Where fan data originates
  • How it is organized and standardized
  • How it moves through the system
  • How it ultimately supports marketing use cases

Core Concepts

Data Sources
Upstream systems that generate fan data, such as ticketing platforms, OneView, Grandstand, and digital properties.

Centralized Data Platform
A shared environment (AEP and RTCDP) that consolidates data from multiple sources for consistent use across Clubs.

Schema
A structured model that defines how fan attributes and behaviors are organized and stored.

Data Ingestion
The process of bringing collected data into Adobe Experience Platform.

Profile Unification
Combining data from multiple systems into a single, continuously updated fan profile.

Segmentation and Activation
Using unified profiles to define audiences and deliver messaging across marketing channels.

The MLB Data Ecosystem

Fan data is generated continuously as fans interact with Clubs, games, and digital properties. These upstream systems produce information about fan attributes (who they are) and fan events (what they do).

Without coordination, this data would remain fragmented across systems. MLB's centralized data platform reduces that fragmentation by ingesting and consolidating data into a shared environment.

Within this ecosystem:

  • Upstream systems generate data
  • AEP ingests and standardizes the data
  • RTCDP enables segmentation and activation
  • Marketing platforms receive evaluated audiences

By centralizing fan data, MLB improves visibility into fan engagement and ensures that information collected in one system can support multiple downstream use cases.

The Role of Data Architecture

While the ecosystem defines where data lives, data architecture defines how it is structured.

MLB's data architecture relies on standardized schemas that ensure fan attributes and behaviors are modeled consistently across systems. When data is ingested into AEP, it is mapped to these predefined schemas. This mapping ensures that ticketing data, digital engagement data, and email interaction data can coexist accurately within a single fan profile.

Because architecture is standardized:

  • Fan profiles behave predictably
  • Segmentation logic performs consistently
  • Use cases scale across Clubs
  • Data quality improves

A clear architectural foundation reduces ambiguity and builds trust in how audiences are defined and evaluated.

Segmentation Evaluation Methods

Answers the questions: How are segments evaluated? What evaluation methods are available? When do segments update?

Segmentation in Real-Time Customer Data Platform (RTCDP) enables Clubs to define, evaluate, and activate audiences using unified fan data. It provides a structured, repeatable workflow that transforms fan profiles into actionable marketing audiences.

Within the MLB ecosystem:

  • Adobe Experience Platform (AEP) manages data ingestion and unified fan profiles.
  • RTCDP allows marketers to define segmentation logic, evaluate audience membership, and activate audiences across channels.

Together, segmentation and evaluation ensure audiences reflect real fan behavior rather than static snapshots, supporting timely and accurate marketing execution.

Core Concepts

Segmentation
The process of defining groups of fans (audiences) based on shared attributes and behaviors.

Audience
The output of segmentation: fan profiles that meet specified criteria and are eligible for activation.

Attributes and Events
Attributes describe a fan (for example, season ticket holder status). Events capture actions a fan takes (for example, purchasing a ticket).

Segment Creation
Defining the logic that determines which fans qualify for an audience.

Segment Evaluation
The process of calculating which fan profiles meet audience criteria.

Evaluation Schedule
The timing and frequency at which segments are recalculated.

Audience Activation
Sending evaluated audiences to marketing destinations.

Audience Readiness
The point at which an audience is fully evaluated and available for activation.

The Segmentation Workflow

Segmentation in RTCDP follows a clear end-to-end workflow:

  1. Segment Creation
  2. Segment Evaluation
  3. Audience Activation

This structured approach ensures audiences are built consistently and can be reused, refined, and scaled across campaigns.

How It Works

Longform (numbered)

Segment Creation

Segmentation begins in RTCDP using unified fan profiles sourced from Adobe Experience Platform.

Marketers define rules using fan attributes and behavioral events. These rules determine the conditions under which a fan qualifies for an audience. Segments can support both simple and advanced use cases, from targeting ticket holders to building behavior-driven audiences.

Once the segment logic is saved, it enters the evaluation stage.

Segment Evaluation

Segment evaluation determines which fans currently meet the defined criteria. During evaluation, RTCDP:

  • Reviews relevant fan profiles
  • Applies the segment's logic
  • Calculates audience membership

Evaluation may occur:

  • On a scheduled basis, or
  • In near real time as new data is ingested

As fan data updates within AEP, RTCDP reevaluates segments automatically. Fans may enter or exit audiences as they meet or no longer meet the defined conditions.

This dynamic evaluation ensures that audiences remain current without manual updates. Understanding the evaluation schedule helps Clubs plan campaigns effectively and avoid delays in audience activation.

Audience Activation

Once a segment has been evaluated and is considered ready, it becomes available for activation. Marketers can send evaluated audiences to destinations such as:

  • Email platforms
  • Paid media platforms
  • Social advertising channels

Because evaluation is ongoing, activation reflects the most up-to-date fan behavior. Over time, segments can be reused, refined, or combined with other audiences to support new campaigns and evolving strategies.

Why This Matters for Clubs

Centralizing segmentation within RTCDP provides several advantages:

  • Reduced reliance on manual list pulls
  • Consistent, repeatable audience creation
  • Automatically updating audience membership
  • Improved campaign timing and accuracy
  • Scalable audience management across seasons

By following a defined segmentation workflow, Clubs can confidently move from raw fan data to personalized marketing execution.
